Parent Setup Guide

Kid Tasker

Complete guide to configuring Kid Tasker and your child's device for daily use.

Overview

How Kid Tasker works

Kid Tasker runs quietly in the background on your child's Android phone. Each morning, the phone is locked until your child completes the day's tasks — chores, a spelling quiz, a math quiz, or any combination you choose. Once everything is done, the phone unlocks for the rest of the day.

A parent PIN protects all settings. The lock resets automatically at midnight so the routine repeats the next day without any action from you.

Before You Begin

Requirements & recommendations

Item Details Status
Android version Android 8.0 (Oreo) or later Required
Accessibility permission Granted once during setup; powers the app blocker Required
Google Family Link Restricts child's access to device Settings, preventing them from disabling Kid Tasker Recommended
Child's Google account Needed to configure Family Link supervision Recommended
Why Family Link?

Kid Tasker cannot prevent a determined child from navigating to Settings → Apps → Kid Tasker → Force Stop and disabling the blocker. Family Link locks the child's account out of device Settings entirely — removing this escape route. The two apps are designed to complement each other.

Step 1 — Device Configuration

Setting up the device with Family Link

Complete this before installing Kid Tasker. If you already use Family Link, skip to Step 2.

Create a supervised account

  1. 1
    On your phone, install Google Family Link from the Play Store and sign in with your Google account.
  2. 2
    Tap Add child and follow the prompts to create or link your child's Google account. If your child is under 13, Google will create a supervised account automatically.
  3. 3
    On the child's phone, sign in with the supervised account. Family Link will install its companion app and take control of the account.

Restrict access to device Settings

  1. 1
    In the Family Link parent app, select your child and tap Controls.
  2. 2
    Under App activity, find and set Settings to Blocked. This prevents the child from opening device Settings at all.
  3. 3
    Under Google Play, enable Approve all apps so your child cannot install new apps without your permission.
Important

After blocking Settings via Family Link, you will need to temporarily unblock it (or approve actions from the parent app) any time Kid Tasker needs to be reconfigured from the child's device. Keep your Family Link parent app handy.

Step 2 — App Setup

Installing and configuring Kid Tasker

Grant the accessibility permission

On the child's phone, open Kid Tasker for the first time. You will be taken directly to the permission screen.

  1. 1
    Tap Open Accessibility Settings. Android will open Settings → Accessibility → Installed Apps.
  2. 2
    Tap Kid Tasker App Blocker, then toggle it on. Android will show a permission warning — tap Allow.
  3. 3
    Return to Kid Tasker. The app will move you into parent settings automatically.
Tip

You only need to grant this permission once. Unless someone explicitly goes back to Accessibility Settings and turns it off, it will remain active across reboots and app updates.

Set your parent PIN

The PIN screen will appear immediately after the accessibility permission is granted. Choose a 4-digit PIN your child does not know. You will need this PIN to access any parent setting in the future.

Don't forget your PIN

There is no PIN recovery mechanism by design. If you forget your PIN, you will need to uninstall and reinstall the app, which resets all settings. Write it down somewhere safe.

Configure your child's tasks

In Parent Settings, tap Task List. The default list includes three starter tasks. Add, remove, or rename tasks to match your household routine.

  • Be specific — "Make bed" is clearer than "Morning routine"
  • Start with 2–4 tasks while your child adjusts to the new routine
  • Tap Save Tasks when done

Enable quizzes (optional)

In Parent Settings, you can add a Spelling Quiz or Math Quiz to the morning unlock sequence. Select your child's grade level and toggle the quiz on. Quizzes draw from a bank of grade-appropriate questions and rotate daily.

Set allowed apps

Tap Allowed Apps to build a whitelist of apps your child can open before the phone is unlocked. This is useful for apps they need in the morning — a homework app, the camera, or a music player.

  • Tap the + button and search for the app by name
  • Tap the trash icon to remove an app from the whitelist
  • Tap Save when done — changes take effect immediately

Exit parent settings

Tap Exit Settings at the bottom of the Parent Settings screen. This saves your configuration and activates the lock. From this point, any app your child opens will redirect to the Kid Tasker unlock flow until the day's tasks are complete.

Day to Day

What the daily routine looks like

What your child sees

When your child picks up the phone and opens any app, Kid Tasker redirects them to the task checklist. They work through each item, then complete any quizzes you've enabled. Once everything is checked off, the phone unlocks and stays unlocked for the rest of the day.

The lock resets at midnight

Kid Tasker resets automatically at midnight each night. No action is needed from you — the next morning, the lock is back in place. You do not need to open the app at all on days when nothing changes.

When you need to change settings

Tap the Parent Settings button on the Kid Tasker home screen and enter your PIN. Any changes you make and save take effect immediately, including while the phone is locked.

Best Practices

Recommendations for a smooth setup

Do a trial run before handing over the phone
Complete the task list yourself at least once to confirm the unlock flow works end-to-end. This is also a good time to verify that your whitelisted apps open correctly before tasks are done.
Use Family Link app approval alongside Kid Tasker
Kid Tasker controls whether the phone is usable each morning. Family Link controls which apps exist on the phone at all. Together, they cover both the daily routine gate and the overall app environment.
Don't over-whitelist
The purpose of the whitelist is to allow a small number of essential apps before tasks are done — not to carve out most of the phone. When in doubt, leave apps off the whitelist and let the unlock flow work as designed.
Keep your PIN somewhere only you can access
A password manager or a note in a locked drawer works well. Avoid anything on the child's device.
Explain the system to your child before you activate it
Kids respond better when they understand the rules ahead of time. Tell them what the tasks are, that they need to complete them to use the phone, and that you can see when it's done. Framing it as a routine rather than a punishment makes a meaningful difference in compliance.
Troubleshooting

Common issues

The app blocker stopped working after a phone restart
This is normal in rare cases where Android terminates the accessibility service after a restart. Open Kid Tasker, tap Parent Settings, then navigate back to Accessibility → Kid Tasker App Blocker and re-enable it. If this happens repeatedly, check that Kid Tasker is excluded from battery optimization under Settings → Battery → Optimize battery usage.
A whitelisted app is still being blocked
Open Allowed Apps, confirm the app is in the list, then tap Save again. Changes are applied when you explicitly save — just adding an app to the list without saving has no effect.
My child's phone shows the task list but the phone is already unlocked
The phone unlocks for the remainder of the day once all tasks are completed. If the child opened the app voluntarily, they will see the completed state. The lock resets automatically at midnight.
I forgot my PIN
There is no PIN reset. Uninstall Kid Tasker from the child's phone, then reinstall it. This resets all settings, including the PIN, and you will go through initial setup again. Your task list and whitelist will also be reset to defaults.
The keyboard is blocked when I try to enter the PIN
This should not occur in current versions. If it does, force-close Kid Tasker from the parent device using Family Link remote controls, then reopen it.
The app blocker was disabled (someone turned off accessibility)
If Family Link is configured correctly, the child cannot reach Accessibility Settings. If the blocker was disabled by someone with Settings access, re-enable it: Settings → Accessibility → Kid Tasker App Blocker → On. Then review your Family Link restrictions.
